04

Choose a forecast method

Rather than assuming a method, I tested four simple ones on Nephron's real series and kept whichever performed best.

Data used

CMS

Medicaid State Drug Utilization

Nephron's quarterly national totals from the Step 02 table, 2022 to 2025.

15

quarters of history, 11 of them forecast

What I did in the code

  1. 1
    Converted the year and quarter labels to numbers, so the code can look back one quarter or one year.
  2. 2
    Set the forecast window to the 11 quarters from 2023 Q1 on, so every method has at least a year of history behind it and all four are scored on the same quarters.
  3. 3
    Wrote four forecasts, each using only earlier quarters: the same quarter last year, a straight-line trend through all earlier quarters, the average of the last two quarters, and last quarter's value.
  4. 4
    Scored each one with the average absolute error (MAE) and the average percentage error (MAPE).

What I found

Average error (MAPE), lower is better

The simplest method was the most accurate

On a volatile 15-quarter series, added complexity did not help. A method that follows the recent trend did worse than one that makes no trend assumption at all.

Last quarter's value was the most accurate, so it becomes the baseline for the comparison in Step 05.

Even the best method is off by about 20% on average. It is a baseline to compare against, not a finished forecast.
