03

Test two hypotheses

Two ideas for what a shortage does to the numbers. I tested each against the Medicaid figures for Nephron and Chartwell.

What I did in the code

  1. 1
    Took the Chartwell and Nephron series from the Step 02 table and kept the quarters where both have usable numbers, dropping Chartwell's suppressed 2024 Q1.
  2. 2
    Compared each quarter's change in the two series and counted how often they moved in the same direction.
  3. 3
    Computed Nephron's mean and standard deviation over all 15 quarters, and a z-score for every quarter.
  4. 4
    Scored the lowest quarter again against the other 14 only (leave-one-out).

What I found

Hypothesis 1 · Substitution

Chartwell and Nephron move together, not opposite

Each square is one quarter. The arrows show the direction of change for Chartwell, then Nephron. If demand were shifting from Nephron to Chartwell, they would mostly move in opposite directions.

QuarterChartwell changeNephron changeSame direction

Chartwell only entered the market in 2024, after Nephron's 2023 Q1 dip, so this covers the period after the shortage, not during it.

Hypothesis 2 · Suppression

2023 Q1 is Nephron's lowest quarter

z-score of each quarter against the 15-quarter average. 2023 Q1 is the lowest at −1.65 (−1.90 against the other 14 quarters). But the lowest of any 15 points typically sits near z = −1.8, so the z-score alone cannot separate it from ordinary variation.

No sign of substitution, and Nephron's lowest quarter is consistent with suppression. Step 06 tests whether that is more than chance.
