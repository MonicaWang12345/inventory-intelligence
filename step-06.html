What I did in the code

  1. 1
    Flagged each of the 11 candidate quarters in turn, re-ran the forecast, and ranked them by how much each one improved the average error.
  2. 2
    Repeated the whole comparison on a second drug, Rifapentine: found its shortage record, found which package code Medicaid reports under, added the two reporting channels, then ran Model C against Model E and the same ranking.
  3. 3
    Added up prescriptions for every drug in each quarter, to see whether 2023 Q1 was low across the whole system.
  4. 4
    Ran the same ranking on a third drug, Lisdexamfetamine (Solco), where the result did not hold.

What I found

Nephron · every candidate quarter

Only 2023 Q1 clearly improves the forecast

Improvement in average error (MAPE points) when each quarter is flagged. Baseline 19.86%; flagging 2023 Q1 gives 18.98%. It ranks #1 of 11. The next best is almost zero, and nine of the other ten make the forecast worse.

Rifapentine · a different drug and manufacturer

The same quarter ranks #1 again

Same method, run on a separate drug without changing anything. Baseline 22.58%; flagging 2023 Q1 gives 20.71%. It ranks #1 of 11, and the next best improves less than half as much.

All Medicaid drugs together

2023 Q1 was the system-wide peak, not a dip

If prescriptions had fallen everywhere in 2023 Q1, the two dips could be a Medicaid-wide data artifact. They did not: total prescriptions peaked that quarter. Nephron and Rifapentine both dipped against the overall trend, which makes each drug's dip more notable, not less. 2023 Q1 is also independently documented as a ten-year high for US drug shortages (ASHP data).

Lisdexamfetamine (Solco) · where it did not hold

An industry-wide allocation shortage behaves differently

+234.33
2023 Q4: the best flag, but a high quarter
−14.86
2024 Q1: the obvious low, ranked last

Numbers are MAPE points. Only nine quarters of data, none before the shortage, and a near-zero 2024 Q1 (3,663 units) inflates the baseline error to 479.54%. This shortage hit the whole industry through allocation, not one company's supply, which is a different mechanism from the one this method is built for.

Three drugs is a small set. With BDM's own transaction data, this boundary could be tested across many more cases.
