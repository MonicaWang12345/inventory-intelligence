Main case · Nephron (Albuterol)

Is 2023 Q1 really special?

The rule skips one flagged quarter. If any quarter would have done as well, the result would be a coincidence. So I flagged every quarter that could serve as a forecast base, one at a time, and measured how the average error changed.

Improvement in average error (MAPE points) when each quarter is flagged. Baseline 19.86%; flagging 2023 Q1 gives 18.98%. It ranks #1 of 11. The next best is almost zero (+0.05), and nine of the other ten make the forecast worse.

Case 2 · Lisdexamfetamine (Solco)

Where it does not hold

What this case is

openFDA has 90 shortage records for Lisdexamfetamine capsules, 33 of them marked Limited Availability, from several manufacturers (Rhodes, Sun, Hikma and Solco among them). Most give the same reason: “Shortage of an active ingredient”. So this is not one company's supply problem. Many manufacturers were short at the same time. I picked it on purpose as a contrasting test.

Which manufacturer I tracked

Solco (package code 43547060410). Its stated reason matches four of the five companies I looked at, so it is representative of the main shortage type here.

What the data looks like

2024 Q1, the obvious low

Units reimbursed per quarter. Only nine quarters, and the first is 2023 Q3. Medicaid records start right around when this shortage was first reported, so there is no “before” period: no normal level to compare against, and none to roll back to. The series also swings widely, from 3,663 to 126,360.

The baseline forecast

QuarterActualForecast (last quarter)Error

Average error (MAPE) is 479.54%. Almost all of that is one quarter: 2024 Q1 is only 3,663 units after 126,360 the quarter before, so a forecast that is off by about 123,000 units shows up as a 3,349.6% error. That is an artifact of MAPE on a series that touches near zero, not a sign the method failed.

The same test as the main case

best flagthe obvious low point

Eight candidate quarters. The pattern is the opposite of the main case. The best quarter to flag is 2023 Q4, a high point (+234.33), while 2024 Q1, the obvious low, ranks last (−14.86).

Why +234.33 does not mean much

Flagging 2023 Q4 changes exactly one forecast: the one for 2024 Q1, which now starts from 2023 Q3 (57,691 units) instead of 2023 Q4 (126,360). Its error falls from 3,349.6% to 1,475.0%, and that single change moves the average by 234 points. It reflects the near-zero 2024 Q1 value, not anything about the shortage.

Verdict

The method does not generalise to allocation-type, industry-wide shortages. That is a real boundary on where the main finding applies. With only nine quarterly points this case cannot say more; daily data is where it could be tested properly.

Case 3 · Rifapentine (Sanofi-Aventis)

The same result on an unrelated drug

What this case is

Priftin (rifapentine) 150 mg tablets, made by a single company, Sanofi-Aventis. The shortage record is fully “Resolved”. A single-manufacturer shortage is structurally closer to the Nephron case than Lisdexamfetamine is.

Finding the right drug code

The product is sold as a carton holding 3 blister packs of 8 tablets each, and each level has its own 11-digit code. I checked both against Medicaid to see which one is actually reported.

LevelCodeMedicaid rowsWhat the rows contain
Outer carton0008821022430All 15 quarters, nothing suppressed
Inner blister pack000882102012Both suppressed

Medicaid reports under the carton code, so that is the one I used. The two reporting channels are added together for each quarter.

What the data looks like

2023 Q1

Units reimbursed per quarter. 2023 Q1 is the lowest of the 15 quarters: 14,219, down from 20,523 the quarter before and back up to 30,662 the quarter after.

Same rule as the main case. Only 2023 Q2 changes, because its forecast used 2023 Q1 as the base: its error falls from 53.6% to 33.1%. Neither model can improve the forecast into 2023 Q1 itself (44.3% either way).

Was it just a Medicaid-wide dip?

2023 Q1

Prescriptions summed over every drug (millions). 2023 Q1 was the system-wide peak, not a dip, which rules out a Medicaid-wide data or policy artifact. Nephron and Rifapentine both dipped against the overall trend. 2023 Q1 is also documented as a ten-year high for US drug shortages overall (ASHP data).

The same test as the main case

Baseline 22.58%; flagging 2023 Q1 gives 20.71%. It ranks #1 of 11 (+1.87). The next best, 2025 Q1, improves less than half as much (+0.79), and seven of the other ten make the forecast worse.

Verdict

The same quarter and the same rule, on a different drug and a different manufacturer, give the same result. That is what makes the Nephron finding more than one lucky series.
